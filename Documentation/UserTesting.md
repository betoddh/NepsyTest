# User Testing

## 1. Testing objective

The team conducted an informal usability test to check whether people could navigate the Nepsy Test main flow, understand the instructions for both modes, and use the Meta Quest 3 controller trigger to complete the activity without assistance.

This was a preliminary usability check of the interface and interaction. It was not a clinical study, a diagnostic assessment, or validation of the test for patients with ADHD.

## 2. Participants and environment

- **Number of participants:** 2.
- **Participants:** One team member's brother and one team member's girlfriend.
- **Device:** Meta Quest 3.
- **Application:** Nepsy Test Unity VR prototype.
- **Testing approach:** Participants used the application on their own and were not given a separate explanation of how to use it.

The participants were convenience testers. The team did not establish that either participant was a psychologist or a person with ADHD, and no demographic or clinical information was collected. Their feedback is therefore useful for basic interaction usability only and should not be generalized to the intended clinical population.

## 3. Tasks completed

Each participant tested the complete application and both modes. The observed task flow was:

1. Start the application.
2. Select Basic mode and read its instructions.
3. Start and complete the Basic activity using the Meta Quest 3 controller trigger.
4. Select Advanced mode and read its instructions.
5. Start and complete the Advanced activity using the controller trigger.
6. Reach and review the final results screen.

The tests focused on whether the participants could follow the in-app navigation and instructions without being coached by the team.

## 4. Results and observations

| Area observed | Result |
|---|---|
| Independent navigation | Both participants understood the on-screen buttons and completed the main flow without assistance. |
| Instruction comprehension | Both participants understood the Basic and Advanced instructions without help. |
| Controller input | Neither participant reported difficulty pressing the trigger. |
| Color identification and response rules | No difficulty identifying the colors or remembering the rules was reported. |
| Aiming | The main point of uncertainty was understanding how to aim at a duck before shooting. |
| Technical reliability | No technical problems were reported during the tests. |
| Overall experience | Both experiences were described as pleasant and enjoyable. |

### Participant feedback

The participants liked the design and visual style. They commented that the game did not feel overstimulating and that the experience was enjoyable. The only point they identified as potentially confusing was how to aim when shooting at a duck.

The team did not record exact completion times, formal satisfaction scores, video-coded behavior, or a standardized usability questionnaire. No numeric usability metric beyond the two participants completing the flow was collected.

## 5. Improvements and iteration

No changes were implemented after this round of testing because the overall feedback was positive and the participants were able to complete the application without assistance. The team did, however, identify aiming guidance as a possible future improvement.

A future iteration could add a short visual cue or a more explicit explanation showing how to point the controller toward a duck before pressing the trigger. This is a recommendation for a future version; it was **not** implemented as part of the tested build.

## 6. Summary

Both participants completed the application and tested both modes independently using Meta Quest 3. They understood the instructions, did not report problems with the trigger or color rules, and encountered no technical issues. They responded positively to the game's visual design and described the experience as enjoyable and not overstimulating. Aiming was the only interaction detail that generated uncertainty. No post-test changes were made.

## 7. Testing limitations

- The sample consisted of two convenience testers rather than a representative sample of psychologists or patients with ADHD.
- The testing assessed basic navigation and interaction, not clinical accuracy, diagnostic validity, or treatment outcomes.
- No formal task-time, error-rate, or satisfaction instrument was used.
- The team did not carry out a second testing round to evaluate an iteration, because no changes were made after this round.
